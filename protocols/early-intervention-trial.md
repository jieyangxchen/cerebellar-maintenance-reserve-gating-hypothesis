# Genotype-Specific Early-Intervention Protocol Concepts for SCA3 and SCA6

**Protocol status:** submission-oriented concept protocols, not treatment recommendations

**Evidence status date:** 2026-08-18

**Intended use:** prospective scientific advice, sponsor discussion, ethics review planning, and protocol development

These modules illustrate how early modifiability of already known molecular or circuit targets could be tested. They do not test or validate the candidate external input, gating function, or reserve flux proposed in the accompanying framework.

## Synopsis and design decision

SCA3 and SCA6 may share a master operational framework, central laboratories, outcome-rater training, imaging acquisition, data standards, and independent safety governance. They must not share a pharmacological treatment arm or a pooled primary efficacy analysis.

The biological reasons are consequential. A mutant-ATXN3-lowering strategy has direct target logic in SCA3. In SCA6, indiscriminate lowering of CACNA1A could reduce an essential calcium-channel function and is therefore not an acceptable extrapolation. Conversely, the completed SCA6 L-arginine study does not establish an intervention for SCA3. Any unknown external maintenance input proposed by the hypothesis remains an unobserved variable and is not an intervention in this programme.

This document therefore defines three modules:

1. **Module A:** a conditional SCA3 Phase 2 trial of a mutant-ATXN3-lowering agent, activated only after SCA3-specific human safety, dose, and target-engagement gates are met;
2. **Module B:** an early-manifest SCA6 Phase 2b trial of L-arginine, using the prior human regimen and a planned withdrawal period;
3. **Module C:** a circuit-targeted Phase 0 module that remains inactive until four strict prospective validation gates are passed.

Modules A and B have separate estimands, statistical families, data-monitoring decisions, and conclusions. Results must be reported by genotype even if the same coordinating infrastructure is used.

## Common regulatory, ethical, and operational framework

### Required authorizations

Before enrolment, each interventional module requires:

- a sponsor with legal control of the investigational product and a valid IND, CTA, or applicable national authorization;
- central and local research-ethics approval;
- prospective registration in a WHO-recognized registry and public posting of the protocol and statistical analysis plan before database lock;
- trial insurance, pharmacovigilance procedures, central randomization, validated electronic data capture, and a written independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) charter;
- product-specific chemistry, manufacturing, and controls documentation and a qualified bioanalytical assay for each pharmacodynamic endpoint.

Publication of this concept does not authorize access to VO659, another proprietary oligonucleotide, or a clinical-grade L-arginine product.

### Participant protections

Only adults capable of informed consent are eligible. Genetic counselling is required before and after confirmatory testing. Consent must separately cover genetic-status disclosure, biobanking, future use, recontact, and controlled sharing of participant-level data. Participants must be told that early intervention can expose them to procedural, pharmacological, employment, insurance, and psychological harms before any proven clinical benefit exists.

Premanifest participants are not eligible merely because they carry a pathogenic expansion. Entry into an intervention requires the disease-specific enrichment and safety gates below. A participant may withdraw at any time without losing clinical care. Pregnancy prevention and pregnancy testing must follow the investigator's brochure and local regulation.

### Concurrent controls

Both drug trials use concurrent randomized controls. Natural-history cohorts may inform design assumptions, stratification, assay qualification, and supportive Bayesian analyses, but they do not replace the randomized control in the primary analysis. Any Bayesian dynamic borrowing must be prespecified, robust to prior-data conflict, and supplemental to the no-borrowing primary analysis.

### Common data-quality safeguards

- Centralized, concealed allocation with randomly varying block sizes;
- intervention-specific matching placebo and separate unblinded pharmacy or dosing personnel;
- blinded central SARA video review and periodic inter-rater calibration;
- scanner qualification, phantom monitoring, identical acquisition parameters within participant, and blinded central image processing;
- collection of treatment guesses from participants and assessors to quantify blinding integrity;
- follow-up after treatment discontinuation whenever consent remains in place;
- one protocol-defined source of truth for visit windows, endpoint derivations, intercurrent events, and analysis populations.

### Independent oversight

The DSMB includes at least one ataxia neurologist, one trial statistician, one pharmacovigilance expert, one bioethicist experienced in predictive genetics, and product-specific expertise. The DSMB receives unblinded safety data; the sponsor team responsible for conduct remains blinded to comparative efficacy. A participant-related death, suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction, or programme-pause criterion is reported immediately under the applicable regulatory timelines.

## Module A: conditional SCA3 mutant-ATXN3-lowering Phase 2 trial

### Scientific question

In biomarker-enriched preataxic or very-early-manifest SCA3, does an allele-preferential mutant-ATXN3-lowering regimen both reduce CSF mutant ATXN3 and slow pontine tissue loss over 104 weeks, with an acceptable safety profile?

This is a biological-activity Phase 2 trial. It is not a registration trial and is not powered to establish delayed disability or phenoconversion.

### Activation gates

Randomization may begin only when all of the following are documented:

1. **SCA3-specific exposure:** at least 12 adults with early-manifest SCA3 have received the intended dose or a pharmacokinetically equivalent exposure in an antecedent study and completed at least 26 weeks of follow-up. Huntington disease data alone are insufficient.
2. **Dose selection:** an RP2D and interval are selected from integrated SCA3 safety, CSF pharmacokinetic, mutant-ATXN3, total/wild-type ATXN3, NfL, and MRI data. No dose is inferred from a press release or another genotype.
3. **Target engagement:** the antecedent SCA3 data meet a prospectively agreed minimum mutant-ATXN3 reduction and show no exposure-related loss of normal ATXN3 function. The numerical threshold is locked with regulators before this protocol opens.
4. **Safety:** no unresolved dose-limiting neuroinflammatory, hepatic, renal, haematological, or imaging signal exists at the RP2D.
5. **Assay readiness:** the CSF mutant-ATXN3 assay is analytically validated for precision, dilutional parallelism, freeze-thaw stability, and longitudinal lot bridging. The MRI core meets prespecified pons-volume repeatability criteria.
6. **Regulatory concurrence:** the sponsor and regulators agree that biomarker-positive preataxic enrolment is ethically supportable after the within-trial sentinel gate below.

As of the evidence date, these gates cannot be declared satisfied from public SCA3 efficacy data.

### Design

- Phase 2, multicentre, randomized, double-blind, matching intrathecal-placebo controlled, parallel group;
- 120 randomized participants, 60 per group;
- target allocation of approximately 60 very-early-manifest and 60 biomarker-positive preataxic participants;
- screening/run-in of 12 weeks, treatment through week 78, primary imaging follow-up at week 104, and off-dose pharmacodynamic follow-up through week 130;
- four intrathecal administrations at day 1 and weeks 26, 52, and 78, using the regulator-agreed RP2D; an exact milligram dose is deliberately not specified before the activation gates are passed;
- no crossover before the week-104 primary endpoint.

### Delayed opening of the preataxic stratum

The trial initially enrols only very-early-manifest participants. Preataxic enrolment opens only after at least 12 active-treated early-manifest participants have completed week 26 and the DSMB confirms all of the following:

- no treatment-related death or programme-pause event;
- no persistent group-level adverse NfL trajectory;
- no inflammatory MRI pattern or sustained CSF pleocytosis attributable to treatment;
- target engagement is present at the RP2D;
- the observed risk remains proportionate to the uncertain benefit of treatment before ataxia.

Failure of this gate closes the preataxic stratum without terminating already-enrolled follow-up.

### Eligibility

#### Inclusion

- age 25-65 years;
- a pathogenic ATXN3 CAG expansion confirmed by an accredited laboratory, with repeat size and assay method recorded;
- one of two disease-stage definitions:
  - **very early manifest:** SARA 3-10 at two visits at least 6 weeks apart; or
  - **biomarker-positive preataxic:** SARA less than 3 at two visits, age- and sex-adjusted plasma NfL z-score at least 2 on two samples 8-12 weeks apart, and either a locked prediction of clinical onset within 10 years or a prespecified abnormal brainstem/diffusion MRI marker;
- stable rehabilitation and symptomatic medication for at least 8 weeks;
- ability to complete lumbar puncture, MRI, digital motor testing, and longitudinal follow-up;
- adequate hepatic, renal, haematological, and coagulation function.

NfL z-score at least 2 is a research enrichment rule, not a qualified surrogate endpoint or a stand-alone indication for treatment. Acute infection, trauma, renal dysfunction, and other plausible causes of NfL elevation must be excluded before confirmation.

#### Exclusion

- another pathogenic neurodegenerative expansion or neurological disorder likely to affect outcomes;
- clinically important brain, spinal, or systemic disease that increases lumbar-puncture risk or disrupts CSF circulation;
- recent major infection, head trauma, stroke, or surgery likely to distort NfL or imaging;
- prior oligonucleotide or siRNA exposure unless product-specific washout and regulator review support eligibility;
- uncontrolled psychiatric illness, active suicidal intent, or inability to provide informed consent;
- pregnancy, breastfeeding, or planned pregnancy during the risk window;
- MRI contraindication, clinically important coagulopathy, or protocol-defined thrombocytopenia.

### Randomization and masking

Randomization is 1:1 through a central system, stratified by disease stage, CAG-repeat band, site, sex, and baseline pons-volume z-score. Allocation uses concealed randomly varying blocks. Participants, treating clinicians other than the procedure team, outcome assessors, imaging analysts, laboratory personnel, and the sponsor's efficacy team remain blinded. Active and placebo groups undergo the same lumbar-puncture schedule and standardized peri-procedural care.

### Visit schedule

- screening weeks -12 and -6: eligibility, repeat SARA, NfL confirmation, genetic counselling, safety laboratory tests;
- MRI at approximately week -8 and baseline to quantify short-term measurement error on the same qualified scanner;
- dosing at day 1 and weeks 26, 52, and 78;
- safety visits or contacts at days 2, 8, and 15 after each administration and at least every 13 weeks;
- CSF pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic collection at baseline and weeks 26, 52, 78, 104, and 130 when ethically and technically feasible; samples at dosing visits are collected before that visit's dose;
- MRI at baseline and weeks 52, 104, and 130;
- clinical, patient-reported, and digital assessments at baseline and weeks 13, 26, 39, 52, 65, 78, 91, 104, and 130.

### Co-primary endpoints and success criterion

Both co-primary endpoints must succeed:

1. the week-26 active-to-placebo geometric-mean ratio of CSF mutant ATXN3 concentration, adjusted for baseline on the log scale;
2. annualized percentage change in centrally segmented pons volume from baseline through week 104.

The trial is positive only if both comparisons favour active treatment and each two-sided test has P less than 0.05. This intersection-union rule does not spend alpha across the co-primary endpoints because both, rather than either, are required. A target-engagement result without a pons-volume result is reported as biological activity without evidence of slowed disease activity.

### Secondary and exploratory endpoints

The fixed-sequence key-secondary hierarchy is:

1. superior and inferior cerebellar peduncle diffusion metrics;
2. cerebellar white-matter, medulla, and superior cerebellar peduncle volumes;
3. SARA slope through week 104;
4. FARS-ADL and PROM-Ataxia;
5. home-video SARA and wearable gait/balance measures;
6. plasma NfL.

Other outcomes include total and wild-type ATXN3, MRS, eye movements, 9-hole peg test, timed walk, quality of life, and time to SARA at least 3 in the preataxic stratum. Phenoconversion is exploratory because few events are expected.

### Estimands

#### Target-engagement estimand

- **Population:** all randomized eligible participants;
- **treatment conditions:** assignment to the RP2D strategy or matching placebo;
- **variable:** log-transformed week-26 CSF mutant ATXN3 concentration, with log baseline concentration included in the model;
- **intercurrent events:** temporary dose holds, discontinuation, and changes in symptomatic therapy use a treatment-policy strategy; data are collected after discontinuation whenever possible;
- **summary:** adjusted active-to-placebo geometric-mean ratio, with percentage difference reported as $100\times(\mathrm{ratio}-1)$.

#### Disease-activity estimand

- **Population:** all randomized eligible participants;
- **treatment conditions:** assignment to the RP2D strategy or matching placebo through week 104;
- **variable:** annualized percentage pons-volume change through week 104 among participants alive at the relevant imaging time, explicitly defining a while-alive disease-activity estimand;
- **intercurrent events:** temporary or permanent treatment discontinuation and add-on symptomatic treatment use a treatment-policy strategy; death terminates the while-alive variable rather than generating an invented MRI value, and disease-related inability to undergo MRI is followed with all feasible clinical outcomes and addressed in sensitivity analyses;
- **summary:** adjusted between-group difference in mean slope.

Supplemental while-on-treatment and per-protocol estimands are reported but do not replace the primary treatment-policy conclusions.

### Statistical analysis

The target-engagement endpoint uses a baseline-adjusted linear model on log-transformed concentrations, with treatment, disease stage, CAG band, site region, sex, and the relevant baseline value. Values below quantification follow a prespecified censored-data rule.

The pons endpoint uses a longitudinal mixed model on log pons volume with continuous time in years, a treatment-by-time term, participant-specific random intercept, and, if supported, random slope. Fixed effects include stage, CAG band, site region, sex, intracranial volume, scanner change, and the two baseline scans; prespecified time terms may model common non-linearity without changing the treatment-by-linear-time primary contrast. The treatment-by-time coefficient is annualized and back-transformed to a percentage slope difference. A categorical-visit model and its adjusted week-104 contrast are supportive sensitivity analyses, not the primary slope test. Scanner changes are avoided; unavoidable changes trigger phantom calibration and a prespecified sensitivity analysis.

Sensitivity analyses include reference-based multiple imputation, jump-to-reference after treatment discontinuation, pattern-mixture models, tipping-point analyses, a per-protocol analysis, and a no-borrowing analysis if supplemental natural-history borrowing is used. A prespecified hierarchical composite sensitivity ranks death first, disease-related imaging inability second, and observed pons slope third, using a generalized pairwise or rank-based comparison. A material reversal between that analysis and the while-alive mean-slope analysis precludes an unqualified positive disease-activity interpretation. Model assumptions, convergence rules, outlier handling, and estimand-aligned missing-data procedures must be finalized before unblinding.

### Sample-size rationale

The longitudinal ESMI analysis reported a biomarker-stage pons-volume sensitivity-to-change ratio of approximately 1.41. As an explicitly approximate planning calculation, multiplying 1.41 by an assumed fractional slowing gives a standardized treatment effect. With 80% power, two-sided alpha 0.05, equal allocation, and 15% attrition, the approximate randomized requirements are:

| Assumed pons-volume slowing | Approximate randomized per group | Approximate total |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 105 | 210 |
| 40% | 59 | 118 |
| 50% | 39 | 78 |

The target of 120 is therefore a Phase 2 scenario centred on an approximately 40% pons-volume effect. The table describes marginal power for the pons endpoint only; it does not establish power for joint co-primary success. Joint power also depends on the agent-specific target-engagement effect, variance, endpoint dependence, and missingness. Final sizing requires simulation from participant-level ESMI or READISCA repeated-measure covariance, expected stage mix, scanner reliability, target-engagement assumptions, and the co-primary success rule.

### Interim decisions and multiplicity

- DSMB safety reviews occur after the first 6 and first 12 active recipients reach the relevant post-dose windows and then at least every 6 months;
- the delayed-preataxic gate is assessed after 12 active early-manifest participants reach week 26;
- one blinded variance re-estimation is allowed at 50% imaging information without examining treatment effects;
- one non-binding futility review is allowed at approximately 50% information. Stopping is considered when conditional power is below 10% and target engagement is below the prospectively locked minimum;
- no early efficacy stop is planned;
- after both co-primary endpoints succeed, key-secondary hypotheses use fixed-sequence gatekeeping. All remaining analyses are explicitly exploratory with confidence intervals or false-discovery-rate control as appropriate.

### Safety monitoring and stopping

Safety includes adverse events, neurological examination, cognition and mood, C-SSRS, post-lumbar-puncture complications, CBC and platelets, PT/INR/aPTT, renal and hepatic chemistry, urinalysis, CSF cells/protein, structural MRI including FLAIR and susceptibility-sensitive sequences, NfL, total ATXN3, and wild-type ATXN3.

Product-specific thresholds are finalized in the investigator's brochure. At minimum, dosing is held pending medical-monitor review for a major platelet decline, clinically important coagulation abnormality, new inflammatory MRI finding, persistent CSF pleocytosis, confirmed unexplained NfL increase greater than 50% from baseline, or a grade 3 or higher neurological or systemic event. Permanent discontinuation is required for a related serious adverse reaction when rechallenge is unsafe, a Hy's-law pattern, pregnancy, or persistent inflammatory CSF/MRI toxicity.

The programme pauses for DSMB review after a treatment-related death, two similar treatment-related grade 3 or higher neurological events, or another predefined aggregate toxicity boundary. Safety stopping does not require statistical significance.

### Reversibility claim

An oligonucleotide with a long CNS half-life is not rapidly reversible. The week-104 to week-130 off-dose period measures persistence and pharmacodynamic rebound; it cannot be presented as proof that acute toxicity or target suppression is immediately reversible. The appropriate term is *dose-adjustable molecular lowering*, not an immediately reversible circuit downshift.

## Module B: early-manifest SCA6 L-arginine Phase 2b trial

### Scientific question

In ambulatory adults with early-manifest polyglutamine-expansion SCA6, does 72 weeks of high-dose oral L-arginine improve or stabilize ataxia compared with matching placebo, and what happens to the treatment difference during a 12-week blinded withdrawal?

This trial tests a known chemical-chaperone candidate. It does not test CACNA1A lowering, an unidentified environmental input, or an unvalidated physical-field intervention.

### Evidence boundary

The completed 40-participant Phase 2 trial enrolled participants with SARA 10 or greater; mean baseline SARA was approximately 15. It reported a 48-week SARA difference of -1.52 points, with a 95% confidence interval from -3.10 to 0.06 and P=0.0582. The trial was exploratory and did not establish efficacy. Serious adverse events occurred in 2 of 20 active participants and 5 of 20 placebo participants. The fatal pneumonia and reversible abnormal-liver-function event in the active group were classified as serious adverse reactions because a causal relationship to study treatment could not be ruled out.

Participants with SARA 3-9 were not studied in that trial. Extending the regimen into this earlier range introduces unresolved floor-effect, placebo-response, adherence, and safety uncertainty; the observed effect in participants with baseline SARA around 15 cannot be assumed to transport to SARA 3-9. The prior result is therefore a planning signal, not a confirmed treatment effect or direct evidence for the full population proposed here.

Premanifest SCA6 carriers are excluded from Module B. Current SCA6 NfL, MRI, and MRS data do not provide a sufficiently validated progression marker to justify exposing asymptomatic adults to this regimen. The ongoing or planned N-acetyl-L-leucine and adaptive-DBS studies address different questions and do not remove this uncertainty.

### Design

- Phase 2b, multicentre, randomized, quadruple-blind, matching-placebo controlled, parallel group;
- 240 participants, 120 per group;
- 72-week treatment period followed by a 12-week blinded withdrawal period;
- active participants switch to matching placebo at week 72; participants originally assigned placebo remain on placebo, preserving outcome-assessor masking through week 84;
- no open-label extension before completion of the withdrawal analysis.

### Eligibility

#### Inclusion

- age 30-80 years;
- pathogenic C-terminal CACNA1A CAG expansion consistent with SCA6, confirmed by an accredited laboratory with repeat size and assay method recorded;
- SARA 3-12 at two visits at least 6 weeks apart;
- gait item at least 1 and ability to walk 10 metres with or without a stable aid;
- reported motor disease duration no longer than 8 years;
- stable rehabilitation, assistive device, and symptomatic medication for at least 8 weeks;
- capacity for consent and completion of study procedures.

Enrolment expands in three prespecified stages:

1. The first 40 randomized participants are restricted to SARA 10-12, the portion of the proposed range that overlaps the prior trial's eligibility.
2. SARA 5-9 opens only after those 40 participants reach the week-12 assessment window, or discontinue before it with disposition known, and the DSMB supports expansion at the Stage 1 gate. A second sentinel cohort of 40 randomized participants with SARA 5-9 is then accrued; SARA 10-12 recruitment may continue, but SARA 3-4 remains closed.
3. SARA 3-4 opens only after the 40-participant SARA 5-9 sentinel cohort likewise reaches the week-12 assessment window, or discontinues before it with disposition known, and the DSMB supports expansion at the separate Stage 2 gate.

Final recruitment is capped at 80 participants in each baseline SARA band (3-4, 5-9, and 10-12), with 1:1 randomization within band. Thus the primary marginal treatment effect is standardized to fixed one-third weights across the three bands, rather than to the realized recruitment mix. After the first 40-person SARA 10-12 sentinel cohort, that band may continue only to its cap of 80. After the 40-person SARA 5-9 sentinel cohort passes Stage 2, all open bands may recruit only to their separate 80-person caps. If a gate does not open, the registered 240-person, three-band estimand cannot be completed; follow-up continues, recruitment pauses, and any narrower-population trial requires a prospective amendment and new power calculation rather than post-hoc reweighting.

At each gate, the DSMB reviews unblinded safety against the module's hold, discontinuation, and programme-pause rules. A gate passes only if **all** of the following locked criteria are met in the relevant 40-person sentinel cohort:

- no treatment-related death, Hy's-law case, or programme-pause trigger; no more than two active participants have a treatment-related serious adverse reaction, and no more than three active participants permanently discontinue because of toxicity;
- at least 85% of all randomized sentinel participants are alive and retained through the week-12 window;
- at least 80% receive at least 80% of planned doses through week 12;
- at least 90% of participants alive and available at week 12 have a centrally scorable SARA assessment, and no more than 10% use protocol-defined rescue treatment before that visit;
- using the forecasting algorithm below, the blinded pooled data give an upper one-sided 80% confidence bound of at most 3.0 points for the within-band SD of SARA change and an upper one-sided 80% confidence bound of at most 15% for the proportion predicted to have SARA 0 or 1 at either primary contrast visit in the next proposed band.

Before each gate, at least 20 consented candidates in the next proposed band complete the same two-visit baseline SARA run-in without study drug. The locked forecast uses their empirical baseline distribution, the blinded sentinel repeated-measures covariance, and externally sourced untreated mean trajectories frozen in the DSMB charter. A 10,000-draw parametric bootstrap from a SARA-bounded longitudinal model produces the two upper one-sided 80% bounds; a treatment contrast is neither estimated nor supplied. Failure of any numerical criterion keeps the next band closed. Passing every criterion permits, but does not compel, expansion: the DSMB may keep a band closed for an unanticipated safety concern, but it may not waive a failed threshold. The model form, random seed, external inputs, definitions, and source datasets must be copied into the DSMB charter before enrolment. No between-group efficacy estimate is used to open a lower-SARA band.

#### Exclusion

- episodic-only EA2, familial hemiplegic migraine without progressive SCA6, or another CACNA1A variant without the SCA6 polyglutamine expansion;
- arginase deficiency, lysinuric protein intolerance, clinically important liver disease, estimated glomerular filtration rate below 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, persistent hyperkalaemia, or clinically important metabolic acidosis;
- unsafe swallowing or aspiration risk not mitigated by a documented plan;
- recent myocardial infarction or unstable cardiovascular disease;
- MMSE 23 or lower, uncontrolled major psychiatric illness, active suicidal intent, or inability to consent;
- pregnancy, breastfeeding, or inability to follow pregnancy-prevention requirements;
- use of an L-arginine-containing supplement within 30 days or participation in another interventional trial within the product-specific washout.

### Randomization and masking

Randomization is 1:1 through a central system, stratified by site region, baseline SARA band (3-4, 5-9, or 10-12), CAG-repeat band, disease duration, sex, and body weight above versus at most 70 kg. Active and placebo granules are matched in appearance, packaging, dosing weight, and schedule. Participants, caregivers, investigators, outcome assessors, and sponsor efficacy personnel remain blinded. Plasma arginine results that could reveal allocation are withheld from the blinded team unless required for safety.

### Dose and treatment schedule

- weeks 0-2: active granule product 0.25 g/kg/day, delivering L-arginine 0.19 g/kg/day, or matching placebo in three divided oral doses;
- weeks 3-72: active granule product 0.50 g/kg/day, delivering L-arginine 0.38 g/kg/day, or matching placebo in three divided oral doses;
- maximum active granule product: 33 g/day, corresponding to no more than 25.08 g/day of L-arginine at the prior formulation's 0.76 g/g content;
- weeks 72-84: all participants receive matching placebo under continued blinding.

Dose is rounded only according to the validated clinical formulation. Dose escalation beyond 0.38 g/kg/day of L-arginine or above 33 g/day of active granule product is prohibited without separate human safety data and a protocol amendment. Adherence is measured by returned packaging, dosing diary, and masked pharmacokinetic sampling.

### Visit schedule

- screening and confirmation at approximately weeks -8 and -2;
- clinic visits at baseline and weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 68, 72, 76, and 84;
- remote safety contact between early titration visits and as clinically indicated;
- blinded SARA video capture at baseline and weeks 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 68, 72, 76, and 84;
- MRI/MRS at baseline, week 36, week 72, and optionally week 84;
- blood safety and pharmacokinetic sampling at each clinic visit, with intensified hepatic, renal, electrolyte, and metabolic monitoring during titration.

### Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the late-treatment SARA contrast through week 72, defined as the equally weighted mean of adjusted change from baseline at weeks 68 and 72. All scheduled observations through week 72 contribute to the repeated-measures covariance model, but they do not change the prespecified `0.5 × week 68 + 0.5 × week 72` contrast. This removes ambiguity and reduces dependence on one visit.

### Key-secondary and exploratory endpoints

The fixed-sequence key-secondary hierarchy is:

1. FARS-ADL change through week 72;
2. PROM-Ataxia and Patient Global Impression of Change;
3. centrally reviewed SARA gait-plus-stance change;
4. digital gait/balance composite and timed up-and-go;
5. cerebellar vermis and white-matter MRS tNAA/myo-inositol and structural MRI measures.

Other exploratory endpoints include 10-metre walk, 9-hole peg test, falls, swallowing, fatigue, quality of life, plasma NfL, and metabolomic or pharmacokinetic measures. MRS is exploratory because current premanifest SCA6 evidence is small and largely cross-sectional.

### Withdrawal analysis and interpretation

The prespecified withdrawal analysis compares change from week 72 to week 84 between original assignments with a treatment-by-withdrawal-time interaction.

A constrained repeated-measures model uses SARA at weeks 72, 76, and 84, original assignment, withdrawal visit, original-assignment-by-visit, baseline SARA, baseline band, and randomization strata. The prespecified contrast is the between-assignment difference in week-84 minus week-72 change; week 76 improves covariance estimation and describes timing without replacing that contrast.

- rapid loss of benefit after active treatment stops supports a reversible symptomatic or state-dependent effect;
- persistence through week 84 supports carryover but does not by itself prove disease modification;
- a disease-modification claim requires concordant durable clinical and biological trajectories in a longer, adequately powered study.

No participant is deliberately worsened to prove reversibility. Rescue treatment remains available under predefined criteria and is handled as an intercurrent event.

The exploratory withdrawal estimand is defined as follows:

- **population:** all randomized eligible participants, analysed by original assignment, who are alive at the relevant withdrawal assessment;
- **treatment conditions:** original assignment to L-arginine through week 72 followed by matching placebo through week 84 versus matching placebo throughout weeks 0-84;
- **variable:** week-84 minus week-72 SARA, using week 76 to improve the repeated-measures covariance estimate but not to redefine the contrast;
- **intercurrent events:** pre-week-72 discontinuation and changes in symptomatic therapy use a treatment-policy strategy; protocol-defined rescue therapy during withdrawal also uses a treatment-policy strategy in the primary exploratory analysis and a hypothetical no-rescue strategy using censoring weights in sensitivity analysis; death terminates the while-alive variable, while loss of walking ability is scored as an observed clinical state where the SARA rules permit;
- **missing data:** all obtainable post-discontinuation and post-rescue assessments are used; missing week-84 data are multiply imputed under arm-specific missing-at-random models using baseline through week-76 history, followed by delta-adjusted and worst-reasonable-case tipping analyses;
- **summary:** adjusted difference between original assignments in mean week-72-to-week-84 SARA change, with a two-sided 95% confidence interval.

Because no continued-active arm exists after week 72, this contrast cannot estimate what would have happened under continued L-arginine and cannot by itself distinguish pharmacological washout, natural fluctuation, carryover, or disease modification.

### Estimand

- **Population:** all randomized eligible early-manifest SCA6 participants;
- **treatment conditions:** assignment to the 72-week L-arginine regimen or matching placebo;
- **variable:** within each baseline SARA band, the equally weighted mean of week-68 and week-72 SARA changes from baseline;
- **intercurrent events:** temporary dose reduction, discontinuation, and changes in symptomatic therapy use a treatment-policy strategy; participants remain under observation after discontinuation;
- **death or disease-related inability to complete SARA:** death terminates the while-alive SARA variable; inability to walk is scored as an observed SARA state rather than missing, while other disease-related nonassessment is handled with remote/video assessment where feasible and prespecified sensitivity analyses;
- **summary:** adjusted active-versus-placebo difference in mean late-treatment change, standardized with fixed one-third weights for the SARA 3-4, 5-9, and 10-12 bands, among participants alive at the relevant assessment.

The withdrawal effect is the separate exploratory estimand defined above and is not substituted for the primary late-treatment estimand.

### Statistical analysis

The primary analysis uses a repeated-measures mixed model with visit, treatment-by-visit, baseline SARA, SARA band, treatment-by-visit-by-SARA-band, CAG band, disease duration, sex, site region, and body-weight stratum. A participant random intercept is included; covariance structure and small-sample degrees-of-freedom method are locked in the SAP. Within each baseline SARA band, the late-treatment contrast is the equally weighted average of the adjusted week-68 and week-72 treatment differences. The primary marginal contrast then averages those three band-specific contrasts with fixed weights of one third each, with a two-sided 95% confidence interval and alpha 0.05. Treatment-by-band heterogeneity is reported with simultaneous intervals and a supportive global interaction test; realized band counts do not redefine the primary weights.

Sensitivity analyses include reference-based multiple imputation, jump-to-reference after active-treatment discontinuation, tipping-point analysis, pattern-mixture models, per-protocol analysis, and models excluding assessments obtained during major intercurrent illness. A prespecified hierarchical composite sensitivity ranks death first, non-death disease-related inability to assess second, and observed SARA change third. A material direction reversal or excess mortality prevents an unqualified efficacy interpretation. A supportive analysis may use compatible natural-history data, but the confirmatory result is the concurrent randomized comparison without borrowing.

### Sample-size sensitivity

Using a common change-score SD of approximately 2.5, 80% power, two-sided alpha 0.05, equal allocation, and 15% attrition gives the following planning scenarios:

| Assumed late-treatment difference | Approximate evaluable per group | Approximate randomized per group | Approximate total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.52 points | 44 | 52 | 104 |
| 1.00 point | 100 | 118 | 236 |
| 0.75 point | 176 | 208 | 416 |

The planned 240 participants round the 1.00-point scenario to 120 per group. This choice is deliberately more conservative than sizing directly from the unstable 40-person pilot estimate. The table is a simple change-score approximation; final simulation must use the prespecified average of weeks 68 and 72, its repeated-measures covariance, exactly 80 participants per baseline SARA band, fixed one-third standardization weights, band-specific floor and dropout behaviour, and plausible treatment-by-band heterogeneity.

The accompanying reproducible fixed-seed planning simulation implements that structure with 10,000 bivariate replicates: 80 randomized participants per band; active-versus-placebo benefits of 0.60/1.00/1.40 points; week-68/week-72 correlations of 0.70/0.75/0.80; change SDs of 2.50/2.80/3.00; approximately 20.2%/15.1%/10.0% dropout by week 72; and observed SARA bounded at 0-40. The fixed one-third-weighted input effect is one point. The simulation estimates 79.96% directional two-sided power (Monte Carlo SE 0.40 percentage point), and a separate null run gives a 5.21% two-sided rejection rate. Floor attenuation yields a mean observed treatment contrast of approximately 0.984 point. This demonstrates internal feasibility only; it is not an empirical power guarantee. Before trial registration, participant-level pilot covariance must replace these illustrative inputs and sensitivity simulations must include weaker low-band effects, informative or treatment-dependent dropout, site effects, and alternative covariance structures.

If the aim were only to detect a 50% slowing of the published mean SCA6 progression of approximately 0.80 SARA point per year, the expected 72-week separation would be approximately 0.60 point. With SD 2.5 and no efficiency gain from repeated measures, approximately 274 evaluable participants per group, 323 randomized per group, or 646 total would be required. Thus Module B is sized around a prespecified one-point signal; it is not a definitive progression-slowing trial unless pilot covariance demonstrates materially higher efficiency.

### Interim decisions and multiplicity

- routine DSMB safety reviews occur after the first 10, 20, and 40 active recipients complete week 12 and then at least every 6 months;
- the Stage 1 expansion gate occurs after the first 40 randomized participants with SARA 10-12 reach the week-12 assessment window or discontinue earlier with disposition known; only passage of every locked safety and blinded-feasibility threshold above permits SARA 5-9 to open;
- the separate Stage 2 expansion gate occurs after the 40 randomized participants in the SARA 5-9 sentinel cohort reach the week-12 assessment window or discontinue earlier with disposition known; only passage of every locked threshold permits SARA 3-4 to open;
- one blinded variance re-estimation occurs at 50% information;
- one non-binding futility analysis occurs at approximately 50% information, with stopping considered when conditional power is below 10%;
- no early efficacy stop is planned because the pilot effect is imprecise and likely vulnerable to regression to the mean;
- the single primary endpoint uses two-sided alpha 0.05. Key-secondary endpoints use fixed-sequence gatekeeping; all others are exploratory.

### Safety monitoring and stopping

Safety assessments include adverse events, swallowing and aspiration screening, pneumonia surveillance, vital signs, weight, CBC, AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, creatinine/eGFR, sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, glucose, urinalysis, and plasma amino acids. Participants receive clear instructions for diarrhoea, dehydration, infection, dyspnoea, jaundice, weakness, and confusion.

Dosing is held for protocol-defined severe gastrointestinal intolerance or dehydration, persistent potassium above 5.5 mmol/L, clinically important acidosis, eGFR decline greater than 30%, or clinically important hepatic-test elevation. Permanent discontinuation is required for a Hy's-law pattern, related serious adverse reaction when rechallenge is unsafe, pregnancy, or recurrent severe metabolic toxicity.

Any treatment-related death pauses the entire module. Two similar treatment-related grade 3 or higher hepatic, metabolic, aspiration, or neurological events also trigger a programme pause and unblinded DSMB review. Restart requires documented DSMB and regulatory concurrence.

### Standard-of-care contingency

If another therapy becomes approved or accepted standard care before Module B starts or while it recruits, the protocol is amended prospectively. Participants must not be denied established care. L-arginine may need to be tested as add-on therapy with the new treatment held stable, or Module B may become scientifically unnecessary.

## Module C: circuit-targeted Phase 0 activation gates

No circuit intervention is selected in this document. The existence of early circuit hyperactivity in an animal model does not establish a directionally interpretable, modifiable human phenotype in SCA3 or SCA6. A sham-controlled Phase 0 study may activate only after all four gates below are passed prospectively.

### Gate 1: reproducible human phenotype

A locked electrophysiological or network-hyperactivity measure must:

- have a prespecified acquisition and analysis pipeline;
- show test-retest intraclass correlation at least 0.75;
- distinguish the intended hyperactive subgroup from controls with externally validated thresholds;
- retain the same direction after adjustment for medication, sleep, fatigue, site, and disease severity.

### Gate 2: temporal and prognostic validity

The abnormality must precede, not merely accompany, worsening. In the prospective cohort it must predict subsequent clinical, imaging, or neurochemical decline after baseline severity and genotype are controlled, and the direction must replicate in an independent cohort.

### Gate 3: directionally selective, human-safe modulation

A human-safe intervention must be shown to move the locked phenotype toward the healthy range without worsening gait, balance, cognition, speech, or another relevant cerebellar function. A protocol that uniformly suppresses activity irrespective of baseline state fails this gate. Dose, stimulation parameters, contraindications, and stopping rules must be supported by prior human safety data.

### Gate 4: washout reversibility without persistent harm

After a prespecified 2-4-week washout, the target-engagement signal must move toward baseline and no persistent neurological loss may remain. This gate distinguishes a controllable functional intervention from structural injury. Failure to reverse or emergence of persistent impairment terminates development of that modulation strategy.

### Conditional Phase 0 design after all gates pass

The activated study would be genotype-specific, randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled, and crossover, enrolling approximately 24-36 phenotype-positive participants per genotype. The primary endpoint would be change in the locked target-engagement measure, not SARA or neuroprotection. A later 48-week study would require new variance estimates and a separate progression endpoint. SCA3 and SCA6 estimates would remain separate unless a prespecified treatment-by-genotype compatibility criterion were met.

## Cross-module reporting and failure criteria

The following outcomes are informative failures rather than reasons to redefine endpoints after unblinding:

- SCA3 target engagement without slower pons loss;
- SCA3 pons change without convincing mutant-ATXN3 target engagement;
- a safety signal that prevents ethically proportionate preataxic exposure;
- SCA6 failure to reproduce a one-point SARA effect despite adequate exposure and retention;
- SCA6 benefit that disappears immediately during withdrawal, which supports symptomatic activity but not disease modification;
- lack of an externally reproducible human hyperactivity phenotype for Module C;
- benefit restricted to an unplanned subgroup without a corrected interaction test.

All results are reported with effect estimates and confidence intervals regardless of statistical significance. Negative and terminated studies remain part of the evidence record. Module-specific conclusions must not be generalized to an unmeasured external input or to hereditary cerebellar degeneration as a single disease.

## Interpretation boundary

These modules evaluate known biological targets and prospectively defined human phenotypes. They do not establish the existence, identity, concentration, or therapeutic value of a hypothetical external maintenance input. Any future intervention directed at a newly identified exposure would require independent identification, toxicology, dose finding, regulatory authorization, and a new protocol.
