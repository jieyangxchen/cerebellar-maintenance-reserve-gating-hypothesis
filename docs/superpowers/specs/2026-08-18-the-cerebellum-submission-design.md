# The Cerebellum Submission Optimization Design

## Objective

Convert the venue-neutral hypothesis package into a manuscript and presubmission package tailored to *The Cerebellum*. The revised article will be presented as a new idea and critical in-depth review, not as original human research, an identified environmental substance, or a ready-to-launch clinical programme.

## Editorial position

The article's central contribution is a nested and falsifiable framework for modifier effects in hereditary cerebellar ataxia. The observable layer asks whether a prospectively frozen exposure score has a reproducible non-linear association with phenoconversion and whether that association differs by genotype. The mechanistic extension asks whether an independently measured activation-supply mismatch depletes a dynamic reserve before clinical transition. Evidence for the first layer does not establish the second.

The title will drop “external maintenance factor,” because no factor has been identified and the body already treats `X` as hypothetical. The preferred title is:

> A Maintenance–Reserve–Gating Framework for Modifier Effects in Hereditary Cerebellar Ataxia

## Relation to prior cerebellar theory

The manuscript will explicitly distinguish its dynamic state `B(t)` from established structural, functional, neural, and cognitive reserve concepts. Mitoma et al.'s cerebellar-reserve consensus and the “Time Is Cerebellum” principle will be treated as foundational prior work. Novelty will be restricted to the proposed activation-supply mismatch, its non-monotonic prediction, the observation model linking reserve to phenoconversion, and the registered tests that can reject those claims.

## Model revision

The manuscript will present two nested layers:

1. **Observable modifier layer:** measured exposure `E`, frozen score `S`, genotype, biomarker trajectories, and phenoconversion. A non-linear association is only an environmental signal.
2. **Mechanistic gating layer:** hypothetical input `X`, activation `A`, uptake `U`, gate `P`, dynamic reserve `B`, and the temporal sequence required to distinguish gating from generic non-linearity.

The reserve equation will be paired with an observation model. Clinical transition is represented either as the first threshold crossing `T = inf{t: B(t) <= B_c}` or as a hazard that increases as reserve approaches the threshold. The text will state sufficient qualitative conditions for low-input idling, intermediate maximal depletion, and high-input rebalancing. Figure 2 remains illustrative and will not be described as evidence of robustness.

## Prospective programme boundary

The main article will retain a concise falsification programme but move operational detail to the public protocols. SCA3 will be framed as the best-supported primary test bed because the motivating environmental association and stronger conversion information are SCA3-specific. SCA6 will be framed as a stringent transport and heterogeneity test rather than automatically pooled evidence. The fixed 0.625/0.375 contrast remains a registered planning option in the protocol, but the article will not present recruitment weights as biological population weights.

Agricultural pesticide intensity and untreated private-well residence will be described as rural-environment candidate operationalizations, not maintenance inputs. High exposure will never be described as beneficial. Outcome-blind exposure reliability and biological calibration are required before either score can be interpreted beyond a specific observational association.

The SCA3 and SCA6 intervention concepts will be summarized as examples of early modifiability of known targets. Full efficacy protocols remain supporting resources because neither drug study tests the unknown input or gating mechanism. Circuit modulation remains inactive until a reproducible human phenotype shows temporal precedence and safe washout reversibility.

## Journal-facing package

The repository will contain:

- a revised English manuscript with no more than a 250-word abstract, 4–6 keywords, no more than three heading levels, and complete declarations;
- a synchronized Chinese working translation;
- evidence-audit and bibliography entries for established cerebellar-reserve theory;
- a presubmission inquiry to the Editor that states article type, novelty, evidentiary boundary, public repository status, and why the article fits *The Cerebellum*;
- a submission checklist recording Word conversion, author address, ORCID, final conflict/funding confirmation, AI disclosure, and preprint transparency items that require author confirmation.

## Acceptance criteria

- The title and abstract do not imply that `X` has been discovered.
- Existing cerebellar-reserve theory is cited and explicitly distinguished from `B(t)`.
- The statistical hump and the gate mechanism are presented as separate evidentiary layers.
- A formal link from reserve dynamics to phenoconversion is stated.
- Rural proxies are not described as nutrients, beneficial exposures, or direct measures of `X`.
- Specific drug protocols are not presented as validation of the framework.
- The abstract is at most 250 words and the keyword list contains 4–6 terms.
- Funding, competing interests, ethics applicability, author contribution, data/code availability, and AI-assistance statements are present without inventing author-supplied facts.
- Repository validation, tests, sample-size generation, figure generation, and whitespace checks pass.
