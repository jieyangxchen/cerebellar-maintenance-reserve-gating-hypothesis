# Evidence Audit for the Maintenance–Reserve–Gating Framework

**Audit date:** 18 August 2026
**Scope:** Core empirical statements, all 25 numbered references in the current manuscript, and additional primary or consensus sources retained to support or qualify related statements in the evidence package.
**Source policy:** Primary research articles, the two explicitly labelled cerebellar-reserve consensus/concept papers, PubMed/PubMed Central, publisher records, and the official ClinicalTrials.gov record were used. No secondary news coverage was used as evidence.

## 1. Purpose and interpretation

This appendix separates three questions that must not be conflated:

1. Is the cited study real and bibliographically accurate?
2. Does the cited study support the local factual statement made in the manuscript?
3. Does the study support the proposed unified variables $X$, $R$, $B$, $P$, or $L$, the outward-leak mechanism, or a hump-shaped exposure-risk curve?

The answer to the third question is currently **no**. The literature supports established concepts of structural and functional cerebellar reserve and several premises that motivate the new extension—age-at-onset heterogeneity, exploratory environmental associations, multicellular pathology, early circuit dysfunction, stage-dependent reversibility in animal models, and candidate early biomarkers. It does not directly demonstrate a single external maintenance input, the proposed dynamic reserve flux, a genetically determined coupling capacity, an exposure-gated exchange channel, a shared latent state, reverse resource leakage, or non-monotonic exposure risk.

No audited research article was flagged as retracted in the checked PubMed or publisher records as of the audit date.

## 2. Claim-tier rules

The tiers describe what language the evidence permits; they are not journal prestige rankings.

| Tier | Evidence class | Permitted language | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1: direct human intervention** | Randomized or otherwise controlled human intervention evidence | “In this trial…”, with sample size, endpoint, direction, and uncertainty | General efficacy, disease modification, or mechanism when the trial was small, symptomatic, heterogeneous, or negative on its primary endpoint |
| **T2: human observational or registry evidence** | Cohort, family series, twin series, ecological analysis, cross-sectional biomarker study, multisite test–retest study, or official trial registry | “Was associated with”, “was observed in”, “the registry lists” | Causality, individual exposure attribution from ecological proxies, validated surrogate status, or proof of a proposed mechanism |
| **T3: preclinical mechanistic evidence** | Mouse, cell, organotypic, structural, iPSC, or non-mammalian model | “In this model”, “supports a model-specific proof of principle” | Human efficacy, clinical reversibility, physiological relevance outside the tested conditions, or a universal SCA mechanism |
| **T4: formal hypothesis only** | Mathematical construct or prediction without direct empirical measurement | “We posit”, “the model predicts”, “a preregistered test would evaluate” | “Evidence shows”, “is”, “causes”, or any presentation as an established biological entity |
| **C1: conceptual prior art** | Consensus, review, or opinion source used to establish an existing definition or research principle | “The framework defines”, “the authors propose”, with the source type explicit | Treating consensus language as direct proof of a new molecular mechanism or clinical efficacy |

### Verdict vocabulary

- **confirmed:** the cited source supports the statement when the study population or model is named.
- **corrected:** the source is valid, but the manuscript’s wording, bibliographic detail, or inference needs revision.
- **unverified:** no direct evidence currently establishes the statement.
- **retract as fact:** the statement may remain only as a hypothesis or prediction, not as a factual claim.
- **current status confirmed; efficacy unverified:** the official registry status is current, but no efficacy conclusion is available.

## 3. Core audited records

### `akcimen2020` — Akçimen et al., 2020

**Verdict:** confirmed, T2.
**Finding:** In a pooled cohort of 786 people with SCA3/MJD from five geographic origins, expanded ATXN3 CAG length had a Pearson $R^2=0.62$ with age at onset. The nine GWAS loci reached only the authors’ suggestive threshold, $P<10^{-5}$, not conventional genome-wide significance.
**Required wording:** “In this pooled 786-participant cohort, expanded ATXN3 CAG length explained approximately 62% of age-at-onset variance.” Do not present 62% as a universal invariant across all SCA3 populations.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7138549/) — DOI: [10.18632/aging.102825](https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.102825)

### `gomez1997` — Gomez et al., 1997

**Verdict:** confirmed, T2.
**Finding:** The report described four SCA6 kindreds. Two large kindreds carried uniform expanded alleles of 22 and 23 repeats; across affected participants, onset ranged from 24 to 63 years.
**Required wording:** “In a four-kindred series, two large kindreds carried uniform 22- or 23-repeat alleles; across affected participants, onset ranged from 24 to 63 years.” This avoids implying that a single family necessarily spanned the full range.
**Source:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9403487/) — DOI: [10.1002/ana.410420616](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.410420616)

### `anderson2002` — Anderson et al., 2002

**Verdict:** corrected, T2.
**Finding:** This was one study of two monozygotic twin pairs—one SCA2 pair and one clinically characterized episodic ataxia type 2 pair. Quantitative differences were found in saccades, postural stability, severity, progression, and inferred regional involvement.
**Required wording:** “A study of two monozygotic twin pairs found quantitative phenotypic discordance consistent with nongermline or external modifiers.” The result does not identify an environmental cause, still less a single external factor.
**Source:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12470184/) — DOI: [10.1001/archneur.59.12.1945](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneur.59.12.1945)

### `martins2025` — Martins et al., 2025

**Verdict:** corrected, T2.
**Finding:** The analysis included 188 symptomatic participants from 109 families in 43 municipalities. Residual age at onset differed by approximately 1.8 years between median-split groups for demographic density, rural-population proportion, and untreated-well-water proportion. These were correlated municipality-level 2010 census proxies, not individual lifetime exposures. Main P values were 0.034–0.046; no continuous dose-response, non-linearity, threshold, candidate chemical, or genotype-by-environment interaction was tested. In one-person-per-family sensitivity analyses, not all associations retained significance.
**Required wording:** “In an exploratory ecological analysis, municipality-level proxies for rural living were associated with approximately 1.8-year earlier residual onset; exposure misclassification, family clustering, correlated proxies, residual confounding, and multiplicity preclude causal interpretation.”
**Mandatory deletion:** Any factual statement that rural residence implies a slightly higher $X$ or $S$.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998691/) — DOI: [10.1002/mdc3.14338](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.14338)

### `zu2004` — Zu et al., 2004

**Verdict:** confirmed within model, T3.
**Finding:** In a conditional, Purkinje-cell-directed SCA1[82Q] transgenic mouse, early transgene suppression produced essentially complete recovery in the measured motor and pathological outcomes; later suppression produced partial recovery.
**Required wording:** “Early suppression of mutant ATXN1 expression reversed measured pathology and motor dysfunction in a conditional SCA1 transgenic mouse.” This is not evidence that early human SCA1 or hereditary cerebellar degeneration is already clinically reversible.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6729947/) — DOI: [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2978-04.2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2978-04.2004)

### `pilotto2023` — Pilotto et al., 2023

**Verdict:** confirmed within model, T3.
**Finding:** In Sca1 mice, early molecular-layer interneuron hyperexcitability preceded overt Purkinje degeneration. Chemogenetic inhibition restored Purkinje calcium signalling; chronic inhibition delayed degeneration and improved motor outcomes. Induced MLIN hyperactivity in control mice produced related pathology. Human support was limited to a shared proteomic signature in patient-derived GABAergic neurons, not in-vivo human circuit causality.
**Required wording:** “In Sca1 mice, MLIN hyperexcitability was a modifiable upstream disease driver.”
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10431915/) — DOI: [10.1016/j.neuron.2023.05.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.05.016)

### `wang2025glud2` — Wang et al., 2025

**Verdict:** corrected, T3.
**Finding:** Purified full-length human GluD2 reconstituted into DPHPC artificial lipid bilayers responded to 10 mM D-serine. Mean open probability was 8.85% at 37°C and 1.03% at 22°C. Ten millimolar GABA also elicited bilayer currents. Wild-type GluD2 ligand-gated currents remained undetected in the tested whole-cell context.
**Required wording:** “In purified hGluD2 reconstituted into artificial lipid bilayers with 10 mM D-serine, open probability was strongly temperature dependent; physiological gating in intact tissue remains to be established.”
**Scope rule:** The result shows that a negative experiment excludes only the tested system and parameter range. It does not weaken all negative evidence or support $X$.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12520249/) — DOI: [10.1038/s41586-025-09610-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09610-x)

### `takeuchi2018` — Takeuchi et al., 2018

**Verdict:** corrected, T3.
**Finding:** Recombinant Cbln1 injected into adult cbln1-null mouse cerebellum transiently improved selected gait parameters. Rapid restoration of PF–Purkinje synapses was principally demonstrated in the earlier Ito-Ishida et al. study and should be cited separately.
**Required wording:** “In adult cbln1-null mice, recombinant Cbln1 transiently improved gait; a separate study demonstrated rapid formation of PF–Purkinje synapses after Cbln1 replacement.”
**Scope rule:** Rescue of a developmental knockout does not establish protein-replacement therapy for polyglutamine SCAs or humans.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5906462/) — DOI: [10.1038/s41598-018-24490-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-24490-0)

### `bell2026` — Bell et al., 2026

**Verdict:** corrected, T3.
**Finding:** The study combined the moonwalker mouse T635A mutation—corresponding to human TRPC3 T573A—with a cerebellum-enriched Δ28 isoform. The combined construct was constitutively active and caused calcium-dependent death in HEK293 cells; pathogenicity was also examined in C. elegans, and structural work identified a candidate inhibitory pocket. Co-expression of Purkinje-enriched PMCA2 improved viability in HEK293 cells.
**Required wording:** “In HEK293 cells, co-expression of PMCA2 partially rescued cell death caused by the hypermorphic TRPC3 construct.”
**Mandatory correction:** Do not describe the PMCA2 rescue as an in-vivo Purkinje-cell experiment or a human treatment result. The structural site is a candidate, not a clinically validated target.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13015894/) — DOI: [10.1126/sciadv.aec9284](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec9284)

### `watanave2019` — Watanave et al., 2019

**Verdict:** confirmed within model, T3.
**Finding:** Pharmacological RORα/γ activation with SR1078 improved behavioural, morphological, and mGluR1-related abnormalities in an adult-mouse cerebellar AAV9-ATXN3[Q89] model.
**Scope rule:** This is a single artificial-expression mouse model, not human efficacy or a genotype-independent maintenance-input therapy.
**Source:** [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2018.10.014) — DOI: 10.1016/j.nbd.2018.10.014

### `hauser2022` — Hauser et al., 2022

**Verdict:** corrected, T3.
**Bibliographic correction:** The volume citation is **2022**, not 2021: *Molecular Therapy: Nucleic Acids*. 2022;27:99–108. The article was published online in 2021.
**Finding:** In patient iPSC-derived neurons, ASOmut4 reduced mutant ataxin-3 by approximately 80% after 10 days while largely preserving wild-type protein; the effect persisted for about four weeks. Selectivity depended on linked SNP c.987G>C and is not universal.
**Scope rule:** This is an in-vitro proof of concept, not human safety or efficacy. It is mechanistically distinct from VO659, which targets CAG-repeat-containing transcripts and must not be described as this SNP-selective ASO.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8649108/) — DOI: [10.1016/j.omtn.2021.11.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omtn.2021.11.015)

### `shen2023` — Shen et al., 2023

**Verdict:** corrected, T2.
**Finding:** The study enrolled 190 participants, but only 60 had genetically diagnosed SCA; the group mixed multiple genotypes and contained 27 SCA3 and one SCA6 participant. Plasma NfL was elevated in the cross-sectional pre-ataxic group and correlated with severity and CAG repeat length. The pre-ataxic sample was small and lacked longitudinal onset prediction.
**Required wording:** “In this mixed-genotype cross-sectional SCA cohort, plasma NfL was elevated in the pre-ataxic group and associated with severity and repeat length.”
**Scope rule:** NfL is a nonspecific injury marker, not a measured latent state and not yet a validated surrogate endpoint for disease modification.
**Source:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37003406/) — DOI: [10.1016/j.nbd.2023.106112](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2023.106112)

### `joers2026` — Joers et al., 2026

**Verdict:** corrected, T2.
**Finding:** Seventy-two participants—18 SCA2, 25 SCA3, and 29 controls—were scanned twice within two weeks at four sites using two vendors. Several MRS measures were reproducible, but concentration estimates required site adjustment for vendor bias, and large SCA2–SCA3 differences required genotype-specific analysis. Participants were early-stage but manifest, not a presymptomatic longitudinal cohort.
**Required wording:** “A multisite short-term test–retest study supports the reproducibility of selected MRS measures after site adjustment and with genotype-specific analysis.”
**Scope rule:** The study does not demonstrate longitudinal change, onset prediction, treatment response, or surrogate-endpoint validity.
**Source:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42260718/) — DOI: [10.1002/acn3.70443](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.70443)

### `berger2025` — Berger et al., 2025

**Verdict:** corrected, T2.
**Finding:** The longitudinal ESMI analysis included 291 SCA3 mutation carriers before and after clinical onset and 121 healthy controls. NfL abnormality was modelled to begin more than 20 years before reported or estimated onset, but stage transitions were few and onset timing was partly imputed. Responsiveness varied by stage; pons volume had the highest sensitivity-to-change ratio across the analysed measures, including in the biomarker and ataxia stages.
**Required wording:** “In the longitudinal ESMI SCA3 cohort, NfL and MRI trajectories were stage dependent, and pons volume had high observational responsiveness.” Do not call pons volume a validated treatment-responsive surrogate or infer clinical benefit from a change in pons volume.
**Scope rule:** The cohort was predominantly European, median follow-up was approximately one year, and only nine carrier-to-biomarker or biomarker-to-ataxia transitions were observed. Observational sensitivity to change does not establish responsiveness to therapy.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12270660/) — DOI: [10.1016/j.lanepe.2025.101339](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2025.101339)

### `ibrahim2026` — Ibrahim et al., 2026

**Verdict:** confirmed within model, T3.
**Finding:** A mouse model carrying the patient-derived SCA44 GRM1 p.Y792C gain-of-function variant developed progressive motor deficits, altered climbing-fibre innervation, and region- and stage-specific Purkinje firing abnormalities.
**Required wording:** “A mouse model carrying a patient-derived SCA44 variant showed that enhanced mGluR1 function can directly drive model-specific pathology.”
**Scope rule:** This does not prove the mechanism in patients or justify suppressing activity across all SCAs.
**Source:** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41525334/) — DOI: [10.1093/brain/awaf477](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaf477)

### `vo659` — ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05822908

**Verdict:** current status confirmed; efficacy unverified, T2.
**Finding:** On the audit date, the official record listed the VO659 Phase 1/2a study as recruiting, with estimated enrolment of 68. It is an open-label multiple-ascending-dose safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetic, and pharmacodynamic study. No results were posted. SCA1/SCA3 participants are aged 25–60 years with SARA 3–18, so this is not a presymptomatic trial. The current record contains five dose cohorts; the protocol has evolved beyond a simple fixed four-dose description.
**Required wording:** “The registry currently lists the trial as recruiting; no registered SCA1/SCA3 efficacy results are available.”
**Source:** [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05822908)

### `ishihara2024` — Ishihara et al., 2024

**Verdict:** corrected, T1.
**Finding:** This multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial assigned 40 genetically confirmed SCA6 participants 1:1 to L-arginine or placebo for 48 weeks. The adjusted between-group SARA difference was −1.52 points (95% CI −3.10 to 0.06; P=0.0582), so the primary endpoint did not reach conventional statistical significance. Eligibility required baseline SARA total score at least 10 and ability to walk 10 m; mean baseline SARA was 15.7 in the active group and 14.8 in the placebo group. Serious adverse events occurred in two active and five placebo participants. The active-group fatal pneumonia and reversible abnormal-liver-function event were classified as serious adverse reactions because a causal association with study drug could not be ruled out.
**Required wording:** “In this 40-participant SCA6 phase 2 trial, the estimated 48-week SARA difference favoured L-arginine but was imprecise and not conventionally significant; efficacy was not established.”
**Scope rule:** The trial does not establish efficacy, disease modification, or safety and efficacy in very-early-manifest participants with SARA below 10. Any proposed SARA 3–12 study must identify SARA 3–9 as an untested population, retain concurrent randomization, and use staged independent safety review.
**Source:** [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11701440/) — DOI: [10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102952](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102952)

## 4. Additional audited records required for defensible wording

### `mitoma2020reserve` — established cerebellar-reserve framework

**Verdict:** confirmed conceptual prior art, C1.
**Finding:** The consensus paper defines cerebellar reserve as the capacity to compensate and restore function in response to pathology, distinguishes structural and functional reserve, and discusses plasticity and environmental enrichment. It provides the required prior framework for any new use of “reserve” in a cerebellar hypothesis.
**Required wording:** “The proposed dynamic reserve $B(t)$ is nested within but not equivalent to established structural and functional cerebellar reserve.” The consensus does not measure $B(t)$, $X$, gating, outward leakage, or a human exposure-risk hump.
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6978293/) — DOI: [10.1007/s12311-019-01091-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12311-019-01091-9)

### `mitoma2018time` — early-intervention principle

**Verdict:** confirmed conceptual prior art, C1.
**Finding:** The editorial proposes “Time Is Cerebellum,” emphasizing treatment while cerebellar reserve remains and before advanced cell loss.
**Required wording:** This source supports the priority of early intervention as a clinical principle. It does not establish reversibility in hereditary SCA, a specific intervention, or the maintenance–reserve–gating mechanism.
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007694/) — DOI: [10.1007/s12311-018-0925-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12311-018-0925-6)

### `meyer2026` — APOE ε4 modifier claim

Meyer et al. enrolled more than 800 people with SCA3. Common APOE haplotypes and individual alleles were not associated with onset. Rare ε4 homozygosity was associated with mean onset approximately six years earlier only in Brazilian participants, and the authors call this initial evidence. The manuscript must include the subgroup and replication caveat.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41854058/) — DOI: [10.1093/hmg/ddag016](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddag016)

### `huang2026` — SCA6 ER-stress resilience claim

Huang et al. identified a six-month UPR/HSP90 response and late CRAC-current/spiking abnormalities in SCA6^84Q/+ mice. The 84Q expansion greatly exceeds the typical human SCA6 pathogenic range, and the authors explicitly caution against unqualified human extrapolation. This is a biological analogy for resilience, not direct validation of reserve $B$.
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12894513/) — DOI: [10.1002/ana.78042](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78042)

### `lee2026` — Oligodendrocyte contribution in SCA1

Lee et al. used an oligodendroglia-specific SCA1 conditional knock-in mouse and found dysregulated myelination, Purkinje axonal shrinkage and torpedoes, and impaired motor coordination. It did not establish Purkinje-cell soma loss or a human causal effect.
[JCI full text](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/195723) — DOI: [10.1172/JCI195723](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI195723)

### `wang2011glia` — Bergmann-glia non-cell-autonomous degeneration

Wang et al. showed that Bergmann-glia-specific APC loss disrupted glial architecture and caused non-cell-autonomous Purkinje degeneration in mice. This supports multicellular causation in a specific experimental perturbation, not a generic SCA mechanism.
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3287075/) — DOI: [10.1002/glia.21154](https://doi.org/10.1002/glia.21154)

### `itoishida2008` — Cbln1 synapse reformation

Ito-Ishida et al. directly demonstrated rapid formation and maintenance of excitatory PF–Purkinje synapses after Cbln1 application in mature cerebellum. It should accompany the 2018 gait-rescue reference whenever synapse reformation is claimed.
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6670322/) — DOI: [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1030-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1030-08.2008)

### `wilke2020` — SCA3-specific pre-ataxic NfL

Wilke et al. studied two independent multicentre SCA3 cohorts and reported NfL elevation approximately 7.5 years before expected onset, with associations to severity and longitudinal progression. This is stronger support for the SCA3-specific pre-ataxic NfL statement than the mixed-genotype Shen cohort, but NfL still remains a candidate rather than a validated surrogate endpoint.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32510847/) — DOI: [10.15252/emmm.201911803](https://doi.org/10.15252/emmm.201911803)

### `benussi2018` and `maas2022` — Cerebellar tDCS trials

Benussi et al. reported benefit in a 20-person randomized crossover trial, but the intervention was a cerebellar-anodal plus spinal-cathodal montage, not isolated cerebellar stimulation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30135258/) — DOI: [10.1212/WNL.0000000000006210](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000006210)

Maas et al. then studied 20 people with SCA3 and found no real-versus-sham benefit on the primary SARA endpoint or cerebellar-brain inhibition after a two-week regimen. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35501469/) — DOI: [10.1007/s13311-022-01231-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13311-022-01231-w)

The treatment section must therefore describe tDCS as exploratory symptomatic neuromodulation. Anodal tDCS is not a demonstrated selective “functional downshift,” and neither study established disease modification.

### `sartorelli2024grid2` — Human GRID2 phenotype range

If the manuscript retains the statement that GRID2-associated ataxia ranges from congenital to mild adult-onset presentations, it should cite a human case-series or case-report source and not infer the spectrum solely from GluD2 channel biophysics.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39312122/) — DOI: [10.1007/s12311-024-01743-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12311-024-01743-5)

## 5. Claims that must be downgraded or corrected

### 5.1 Formal constructs with no direct evidence

The following must be labelled as theoretical constructs throughout the title, abstract, equations, figures, results-oriented prose, and protocol:

- external maintenance factor $X$;
- genetically specified coupling or demand vector $R$;
- initial or effective reserve $B_0$ / $B$;
- environmental exchange gate $P$;
- shared latent system state $L$;
- outward leakage after gate opening;
- low-$X$ dormancy as a protective state;
- a hump-shaped or multimodal exposure-risk curve.

Allowed verbs are **posit**, **define**, **hypothesize**, **predict**, and **test**. Disallowed verbs are **show**, **demonstrate**, **establish**, and **cause**, unless the sentence refers only to a directly measured result in a named study model.

### 5.2 Rural-environment interpretation

Replace any causal or X-specific account with:

> An exploratory ecological study identified associations between municipality-level rural proxies and residual age at onset. These data neither identify an exposure nor establish causality, non-linearity, a gating mechanism, or an external maintenance factor; they motivate prospective individual-level exposure measurement and preregistered comparison of linear and non-linear models.

### 5.3 Reversibility

Replace broad statements such as “early hereditary cerebellar degeneration is reversible” with:

> Conditional SCA1 and circuit-manipulation mouse studies demonstrate stage-dependent reversibility or delay of selected functional and pathological outcomes, providing a preclinical rationale to test earlier intervention in humans.

### 5.4 TRPC3 calcium rescue

Replace “enhancing Purkinje-cell calcium clearance improved survival” with:

> In HEK293 cells expressing a hypermorphic TRPC3 construct, co-expression of the Purkinje-enriched calcium pump PMCA2 improved cell viability.

### 5.5 Biomarker claims

Replace “NfL and MRS define the hidden state” with:

> NfL and selected MRS measures are candidate indicators that may contribute to a prespecified latent-variable model; the latent construct, measurement invariance, longitudinal prediction, and treatment responsiveness require external validation.

Neither NfL nor MRS is currently a validated surrogate for disease modification. A trial may include them as mechanistic or key secondary endpoints while retaining independent clinical and functional endpoints.

### 5.6 Neuromodulation

Remove the characterization of cerebellar anodal tDCS as a proven inhibitory “downshift.” The positive heterogeneous-ataxia trial and negative SCA3 trial should be presented together. Any early-intervention protocol should first establish the target network phenotype and the direction, reproducibility, and safety of modulation.

### 5.7 VO659

The manuscript must distinguish trial existence from therapeutic efficacy:

> VO659 is being evaluated in an open-label Phase 1/2a safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetic, and pharmacodynamic study. The official registry currently lists recruitment and contains no SCA efficacy results.

## 6. Defensible synthesis

A submission-safe summary is:

> Current evidence supports several premises of the framework—substantial residual age-at-onset heterogeneity and candidate preataxic biomarkers in selected human cohorts, exploratory environmental associations, and multicellular contributions, early circuit/homeostatic abnormalities, or stage-dependent reversibility in selected animal or cellular models. No study yet directly demonstrates a single external maintenance factor, the proposed reserve and gating variables, outward resource leakage, or a non-monotonic exposure-risk function. These elements are therefore explicit, falsifiable theoretical constructs rather than established disease mechanisms.
